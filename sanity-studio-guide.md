# Studio Content Guide
### How to update the Student Council website

Welcome! This guide explains how to use the Sanity Studio to manage content on the website. You don't need to know how to code — just follow the steps below for each type of content.

---

## Getting Started

1. Open your browser and go to the Studio URL (your developer will provide this)
2. Log in with your account
3. In the left sidebar, you'll see three content types: **Blogs**, **Gallery**, and **Members**
4. Click any item in the list to edit it, or click **+ Create** (or the pencil icon) to add something new
5. When you're done editing, click the **Publish** button in the bottom right corner — **content is not live until you publish it**

> **Important:** Always click **Publish** after making changes. If you only click Save, the changes won't appear on the website.

---

## Blogs

> **Where this appears on the website:** The **Blog** page (`/blog`) and each individual blog post page (`/blog/[post]`)

Each Blog document is one article written by a council member.

---

### Title of blog
**What to enter:** The full headline of your blog post.

**Where it appears:**
- As the large heading on the blog card in the blog list
- As the large title at the top of the blog post page
- In the browser tab when someone reads the post

**Tips:**
- Keep it punchy and descriptive
- Don't make it too long — it displays at a large size on the card

---

### Author
**What to enter:** The full name of the person who wrote the post.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown below the title on the post page as "By [Name]"

**Tips:**
- Use the member's full name as it appears on the Members page for consistency

---

### Category
**What to enter:** Select one category from the dropdown list.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown as a small tag label on both the blog list card and the post page header
- Used to filter posts on the Blog page (visitors can click a category button to filter)

**Available categories:**

| Category | Use it when… |
|---|---|
| Event/Programme | The post is about a specific school event or programme |
| Personal | A personal reflection or story from the author |
| Thematic | Exploring a broader topic or idea |
| Record | Documenting something that happened |
| Experience/Anecdote | Sharing a personal experience or story |
| Discussion | Presenting an argument, debate, or viewpoint |
| Case Study | An in-depth look at a specific situation |
| Behind-the-Scenes | What happened backstage or in the planning |
| Recap | A summary of what happened at an event |
| Review | Reviewing a product, book, event, or experience |
| Reflective | Looking back on something with reflection |
| Informative | Sharing useful information or knowledge |
| Analysis | A deeper analytical look at a topic |
| Relatable | A post meant to resonate personally with readers |

---

### Published at
**What to enter:** The date and time you want the post to appear as "published." Defaults to right now.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown on the blog card and post page as the publish date (e.g., "Apr 06, 2026")
- Controls the sort order — the most recently published post appears first on the blog list

**Tips:**
- You can set a future date, but the post will still be live immediately once published — this date is just displayed as a label
- If you want to backdate a post (e.g., writing about an event that happened last week), change this date to match the event date

---

### Image for the cover of your article
**What to enter:** Upload a photo to use as the blog post's featured image.

**Where it appears:**
- As a large full-width banner at the top of the blog post page (takes up most of the screen)
- As the card image on the blog list page

**Tips:**
- Use a landscape/horizontal photo for best results (the hero image is wider than it is tall)
- Aim for a high-quality image — it's the first thing people see
- The image will be slightly darkened with an overlay so white or very light photos may lose contrast

---

### Description of blog
**What to enter:** A short 1–3 sentence summary or teaser of your post.

**Where it appears:**
- As the excerpt text visible on the blog list card (under the title)
- As a styled intro paragraph at the top of the blog post page, displayed in a large handwritten font in blue

**Tips:**
- Write it like a teaser — give just enough to make someone want to click and read more
- Keep it under 3 sentences; it will overflow on the card if it's too long

---

### Blog (Article Body)
**What to enter:** The full article content. This is a rich text editor.

**Where it appears:**
- As the main article body on the blog post page, below the title card

**Formatting options available:**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Normal text** | Standard paragraph — the main body of your writing |
| **H1 / H2 / H3** | Section headings in decreasing sizes |
| **Bold** | Makes text **bold** |
| **Italic** | Makes text *italic* |
| **Bullet list** | A bulleted list |
| **Numbered list** | A numbered list |
| **Blockquote** | Highlights a quote — appears in a large blue handwritten style |
| **Link** | Attach a hyperlink to selected text |
| **Image** | Insert an image inline within the article |

**Tips:**
- Use **H2** as your main section headings and **H3** for sub-sections within those
- Use **Blockquote** for impactful quotes or pull quotes — they stand out visually
- You can insert images anywhere in the body by clicking the image icon in the toolbar — these will appear as polaroid-style framed photos
- Keep paragraphs concise — large walls of text are hard to read on screen

## Gallery

> **Where this appears on the website:** The **Gallery** page (`/gallery`) and each individual event gallery page (`/gallery/[event]`)

Each Gallery document is one school event with photos arranged into categories. Events are grouped by school term.

---

### Title of event
**What to enter:** The full name of the school event.

**Where it appears:**
- As the large heading on the gallery list page next to the event photo
- As the large title on the individual event gallery page
- In the browser tab when someone views the event

---

### Description of event
**What to enter:** 1–3 sentences describing what the event was about.

**Where it appears:**
- As the body text next to the event photo on the gallery list page
- As a styled paragraph in the handwritten font on the individual event page

**Tips:**
- Describe the event in a warm, engaging way — not just "This is X event"
- It doesn't need to be a full recap; the photos tell the story

---

### Date of event
**What to enter:** Select the actual date the event took place.

**Where it appears:**
- As a small date label (with a calendar icon) on both the gallery list and event page
- Used to sort events in chronological order within each term on the Gallery page

> **Important:** This is the date the event happened, not the date you're uploading the photos. Events are ordered by this date.

---

### Term of event
**What to enter:** Select which school term the event belongs to.

**Where it appears:**
- Determines which section the event appears in on the Gallery page
- The gallery is grouped into 4 sections: **Term 1, Term 2, Term 3, Term 4**

| Value | Displayed As |
|---|---|
| Term 1 | Term 1 — "New Beginnings" |
| Term 2 | Term 2 — "Building Momentum" |
| Term 3 | Term 3 — "Spring Awakening" |
| Term 4 | Term 4 — "The Grand Finale" |

> **Important:** If you assign the wrong term, the event will appear under the wrong section on the website.

---

### 2 words to describe the event
**What to enter:** A very short phrase (ideally 2 words, maximum 20 characters) that captures the vibe or theme of the event.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown as a small coloured sticker label overlapping the event's photo on the gallery list page

**Tips:**
- Keep it punchy and fun: "Pure Fun", "We Did It", "Getting Lit", "Squad Goals"
- Avoid full sentences — this is a label, not a caption
- Maximum 20 characters total

---

### Cover Photo
**What to enter:** Upload the single best photo representing this event.

**Where it appears:**
- As the main photo shown on the gallery list alongside the event description
- As the large polaroid-style photo on the individual event gallery page

**Tips:**
- Choose a photo that shows the energy and scale of the event — ideally with people in it
- Landscape (wider than tall) photos work best
- You can set a **hotspot** (click the image after uploading, then drag the circle to your subject) so the photo crops correctly at all sizes — this is especially useful if you want a specific person or object to always stay visible

---

### Highlights *(Required)*
**What to enter:** Upload the best photos from the event — the ones most worth seeing.

**Where it appears:**
- This is the **first tab** shown when someone opens the event gallery page
- Displayed as a grid of polaroid-style photos, 9 at a time (with a "Load More" button if there are more than 9)

**Tips:**
- Upload at least 3–5 photos; a single photo looks sparse
- These are your headline shots — pick the most visually striking ones
- The website automatically adds a slight decorative tape effect to each photo

---

### BTS *(Optional)*
**What to enter:** Behind-the-scenes photos — preparation, candid moments, unpolished shots.

**Where it appears:**
- As a separate **"Behind the Scenes"** tab on the event gallery page (only appears if you upload at least one photo)

**Tips:**
- Great for showing the effort and process behind events, not just the polished result

---

### Student's Collections *(Optional)*
**What to enter:** Photos taken or submitted by students at the event.

**Where it appears:**
- As a **"Students Collections"** tab on the event gallery page (only appears if you upload at least one photo)

---

### Miscellaneous *(Optional)*
**What to enter:** Any other photos that don't fit the categories above.

**Where it appears:**
- As a **"Miscellaneous"** tab on the event gallery page (only appears if you upload at least one photo)

**Tips:**
- If a photo category has no images uploaded, that tab will not appear on the website at all — so don't worry about leaving optional categories empty

## Members

> **Where this appears on the website:** The **Members** page (`/members`)

Each Members document is one council member. The page is divided into two sections:
- **The Council** — major leadership positions (StuCo Advisor through Public Relations Officer)
- **Level Representatives** — one rep for each school level

---

### Name
**What to enter:** The full name of the council member.

**Where it appears:**
- On their member card on the Members page
- As part of their anchor navigation (the sidebar index that lets visitors jump to each member)

---

### Role
**What to enter:** Select this member's position from the dropdown list.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown as a label on their member card
- Determines **which section of the page** the member appears in, and **in what order**

> **⚠️ This is the most critical field.** The position must exactly match one of the options in the dropdown. Each position should only have **one member** — if you create two members with the same position, only one will appear on the website.

**Positions and where they appear:**

| Position | Section | Display Order |
|---|---|---|
| StuCo Advisor | The Council | 1st |
| President | The Council | 2nd |
| Vice-President | The Council | 3rd |
| Secretary General | The Council | 4th |
| Finance and Logistics Officer | The Council | 5th |
| Public Relations Officer | The Council | 6th |
| JC2 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 1st |
| JC1 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 2nd |
| Sec 4 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 3rd |
| Sec 3 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 4th |
| Sec 2 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 5th |
| Sec 1 Level Representative | Level Representatives | 6th |

---

### 2 words to describe you
**What to enter:** A short self-description from the member (maximum 25 characters).

**Where it appears:**
- Shown as a small coloured sticker overlapping the member's photo card

**Tips:**
- Should feel personal and fun: "Always Smiling", "Chaos Mom", "Detail King", "Hype Woman"
- Keep it short — this is a sticker label, not a bio
- Maximum 25 characters

---

### Image
**What to enter:** A photo of the council member.

**Where it appears:**
- As the main photo on their member card

**Tips:**
- Use a clear photo of just the person, ideally from the shoulders up
- Portrait orientation (taller than wide) works best, but any photo will be cropped
- After uploading, click the photo and **drag the hotspot circle** onto the member's face so they always crop in correctly at all sizes

---

### Description
**What to enter:** A short personal bio (minimum 5 characters, maximum 200 characters).

**Where it appears:**
- As a paragraph of body text on their member card

**Tips:**
- Write it in first person ("I love…") or third person ("She is the…") — be consistent with other members
- 200 characters is roughly 2–3 short sentences — keep it brief
- Share something personal, not just their role

---

### Events
**What to enter:** A list of events this member was involved in or helped organise. Click **Add item** and type each event name separately.

**Where it appears:**
- Shown as a small list of tags/labels on their member card

**Tips:**
- Enter each event as a separate item (e.g., one item for "Orientation", another for "Sports Day")
- This is optional — if you leave it blank, no event tags will appear on the card
- Match the event name closely to what it's called on the Gallery page for consistency

## Quick Reference: Common Tasks

### Add a new blog post
1. Click **Blogs** in the sidebar → **+ Create**
2. Fill in: Title, Author, Category, Published at date, Cover Image, Description, and Body
3. Click **Publish**

### Add a new gallery event
1. Click **Gallery** in the sidebar → **+ Create**
2. Fill in: Title, Description, Date, Term, Short Words, Cover Photo, and Highlights (required)
3. Optionally add BTS, Students Collections, or Miscellaneous photos
4. Click **Publish**

### Update a member
1. Click **Members** in the sidebar
2. Find and click the member you want to edit
3. Make your changes
4. Click **Publish**

### Replace a member's photo
1. Open their member document
2. Click the current image → click **Replace** or **Remove** and re-upload
3. Drag the hotspot to their face after uploading
4. Click **Publish**

### Change the publish date of a blog post
1. Open the blog document
2. Click the **Published at** field and update the date
3. Click **Publish** — the post will now appear with the new date and may reorder in the blog list

## Tips & Gotchas

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| I published something but it's not showing on the website | Wait 10–30 seconds and refresh the website. Changes take a moment to appear. |
| I can see my changes in the Studio but the website still shows old content | Hard refresh the website page (Ctrl+Shift+R on Windows, Cmd+Shift+R on Mac) |
| A member is missing from the Members page | Check that their **Role** field is exactly one of the accepted positions in the dropdown |
| An event is missing from the Gallery page | Check that the **Term** field is set correctly |
| The cover photo looks cropped in a weird way | Edit the image in the Studio and move the **hotspot** circle to the most important part of the photo |
| A blog post category isn't filtering correctly | Make sure the **Category** field was set using the dropdown, not typed manually |
| I accidentally deleted something | Contact your developer — Sanity keeps a history of all changes and content can usually be restored |
